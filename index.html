Head · Lead · Staff Product Design

I design products and organisations.

I design complex B2B and SaaS products. I specialize in data-heavy interfaces and expert user flows.

Twenty-five years and dozens of products — telecom platforms, broadcast services, dealer systems, meat-industry ERPs, emergency care triage simulations. A design function built from zero, an operating model that outlived me, and an AI-assisted design practice now used across a media group.

I work best where the business itself is hard: regulated, data-heavy, expert users, decades of legacy nobody dares touch. I stay in the detail because in domains like these the detail is the strategy — the definitions and the screens are still mine.

Currently

Design lead on a SaaS platform replacing three legacy systems across 400+ dealerships, and one of two designers representing product design on Alma's group-wide Core AI team.

    Member of Alma's Core AI team

About

I work in three languages.

Design is my strongest one. But before design, I started in front-end and full-stack engineering: Java and C# as a client-side developer, LAMP work for payment gateways, and single-sign-on integrations. It's why I could be handed direct control of a telecom's front-end teams to save a launch date, and why I build my own tooling rather than waiting for someone to ship it. Business I studied and then lived: an M.Sc. in economics with a focus on organisational theory, and twenty years since then in strategy rooms, PRDs, and business definitions.

I discuss implementation costs directly with engineering. I argue strategy using business terminology.

Most of my career has been in enterprise and B2B: telecom BSS, broadcast, dealer management, industrial ERPs. I work in domains where users are experts. Existing practices often look strange but serve specific operational needs. I prioritize understanding these workflows before altering them.

How I work

At Onesto and Qvantel we built an operating model we called enterprise design, on two ideas. Business, design, and technology contributed equally to the roadmap. We prioritized requirements based on user data. And experience runs in layers: usability at the core, service design around it, and around that the experience of employees, support staff, and stakeholders. Support staff working against a system they hate cannot deliver good support. Ordinary enough now; distinctly uncommon in 2012.

I'm doing a version of the same thing again with AI-assisted design: building the practice, then getting people to use it.

Based in Jyväskylä, Finland. I work in Finnish and English.

I'm open to head, lead, and staff product design roles.
